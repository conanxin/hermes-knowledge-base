摘要：Nick Land 的加速主义哲学究竟是什么？

一句话概括

本文通过细读尼克·兰从 1988 到 1990 年代中后期的核心文本，论证所谓"加速主义"既非克赖斯特彻奇枪手式的白人至上恐怖策略，亦非硅谷 e/acc 派对资本主义的乐观礼赞，而是兰借死亡与德勒兹—瓜塔里的解码化框架所构筑的、一种以人类之非人化与灭绝为旨归的反人类中心主义哲学。

详细摘要

尼克·兰的智识轨迹可分为两阶段：早期（1988–1992）以反种族隔离、反康德、反现象学的批判为主线；晚期（1993–1995）则在拥抱技术资本主义的同时，将这一批判推到其最激进的极端——以人类中心主义的灭亡本身为目的。本文核心论证在于，这两阶段共享同一条主轴：兰的全部关切在于击破人类对自身之宇宙中心地位的拟人化幻想。

在哲学谱系上，兰从康德的先验唯心论出发，借用其"现象—本体"之分，但将这一区分倒转：本体界不是不可知的边界，而为资本主义帝国主义所构造、所压抑的他者。种族隔离的"班图斯坦"机制，正是兰所诊断的西方现代性之微观缩影。兰进一步将康德与现象学传统——胡塞尔、海德格尔、黑格尔、德里达——一并归入"人类中心主义傲慢"行列：它们共同的失败在于无法直面死亡，于是让哲学继续在人的镜像中打转。

早期兰以情欲唯物主义（libidinal materialism）作为替代方案，将死亡立为评判哲学可信度的客观判准，并倚重叔本华、尼采、弗洛伊德、巴塔耶等思想资源。1993 年的《与死共造》之后，兰发生决定性转向：资本主义的技术创新——尤其人工超级智能——成为比任何人类起义都更具毁灭性的、能将外部（Outside）内爆于人类之中的力量。

此转向深受德勒兹与瓜塔里《反俄狄浦斯》的启发，但兰对其有所取舍：他接受了资本主义对欲望流的彻底解码化、对一切固定身份价值的消解，却进一步将其推至德勒兹不敢走到之处——肯定资本主义的逻辑后果，包括人类本身之被废除。在他看来，技术奇点（singularity）正是"无器官身体"（Body without Organs）的终极化身：它要求人类在被一种超越其认知之超级智能所面对时，承认自身认知的限度。

兰的立场因此既同于反资本主义者——他们都视资本主义为深度非人化之机器；又同于安·兰意义上的"末日论者"——他们都承认 AI 对人类存续的威胁。兰与他们的差异仅在于：他不认为应去阻挠这一进程，而认为应当正面肯定之。对他而言，人类的完善与繁荣并非目的；对真实的绝对知识——哪怕是非人性的——才是目的。本文作者最后指出：这恰好是当下两派自称兰之信徒者共同误读的关键所在：他们皆在兰的彻底非人道主义中偷偷塞入了人类中心主义的残余梦想。

哲学背景

要理解兰的哲学轨迹，必须将其放回后康德以来欧陆哲学的几条主轴之中。在唯心论与唯物论之对峙中——黑格尔的绝对精神、胡塞尔的现象学、海德格尔的存在论——人类主体性始终被置于意义生成之中心；与之相对，叔本华以降的意志哲学、尼采的悲剧—命运哲学、弗洛伊德—巴塔耶的死亡驱力理论，则开辟了一种将人类降格为非人之力量之载体的反人类中心主义传统。兰站在这一传统的延长线上，但又将其向前推进至极端：他不仅否定人是意义的源头，更激进地肯定这一否定。后结构主义（尤其德勒兹与瓜塔里）则为兰提供了将资本主义整体重述为欲望流之解码化—再编码机制的当代工具。重要者，这一发展正发生在两个截然不同的历史语境之夹缝中：1960 年代末的起义氛围（《反俄狄浦斯》成书于此时）曾赋予德勒兹与瓜塔里某种对革命主体的乐观，而兰阅读他们的 1990 年代——柏林墙倒塌、撒切尔/里根时代终结、新自由主义全球扩张——则将一种彻底冷峻的、非人类的尺度注入了同一批概念中。

文体/方法论意义

本文在文体上采取一种文本细读式的哲学史考察，与过去十年加速主义之流行讨论形成鲜明对照：它不依赖二手综述、博客帖文或政治评论，而是回到兰与德勒兹—瓜塔里、康德、巴塔耶的原始文本之中，逐段比对、辨析。这种细读带来一种双重矫正：既矫正白人至上加速主义者对兰的庸俗挪用（他们抽走了兰对一切人类价值之亵渎，仅留下"加速崩溃"这一战术性姿态），又矫正 e/acc 派对兰的乐观挪用（他们抽走了兰对一切人类之肯定性灭绝，仅留下对资本主义的修辞性礼赞）。作者由此重塑了兰这一形象——既非恐怖分子，亦非技术福音派，而是一位最终将资本主义与反资本主义一同以"非人之真实"之名牺牲掉的、激进的反人类中心主义哲学家。

值得继续研究的问题

  • 为什么 Land 在 1990 年代后期转向 NRx / 新反动右翼，而其哲学内核却从未改变？兰后来对种族、民族国家、君主制的公开肯定，与其早期反种族隔离批判之间的张力，应如何从其关于非人类中心之根本立场加以重估？
  • 德勒兹与瓜塔里的《反俄狄浦斯》中，兰具体拒绝了哪些保留意见？这些保留意见——特别是资本主义仍依赖家庭与国家之再生产机制——在今天看来是否反而更具说服力？
  • "情欲唯物主义"（libidinal materialism）与今天加速主义研究中常被引用的德勒兹解读有什么关键差别？兰将巴塔耶、弗洛伊德的死亡驱力置于何种位置，而德勒兹—瓜塔里又为何要削弱这一脉？
  • 人工智能奇点是否真的能扮演兰所说的"无器官身体"角色？或者这本身是一种隐喻性的偏移——将兰哲学中的崇高 X 翻译成当代 AI 议题时，是否发生了某种意义的滑移？
  • 当代 e/acc 与 1990 年代 Land 的共同点到底是什么？——除了对市场的修辞性狂热之外，硅谷的技术乐观主义究竟在多大程度上真正延续了兰的反人类中心主义内核，又在多大程度上将其偷偷替换为一种人本主义的天堂想象？

Nick Land 的加速主义哲学究竟是什么？

全文约 6,100 字。Vincent Lê 撰文；编辑：Cameron Allan McKean；插图：Martin O'Neill / 《Cut it Out》。 2026 年 6 月 11 日发表于 Aeon Essays。 主题：思想史 · 计算与人工智能 · 思想家与理论。

无人的未来

从恐怖分子到科技巨头，各色人等都将加速主义视作革命的武器。尼克·兰所窥见的，则是一片远为幽暗的图景

这个词频繁出现在恐怖主义报告与科技公司发布会中，出现在大规模枪击案凶手的宣言里，也出现在亿万富翁的公开声明中。"加速主义"究竟是什么？过去十年——尤其是最近几年，这个术语已从互联网的阴暗角落迁入主流政治与文化，并在此过程中分裂为两种彼此水火不容的支配性形态。一派加速主义者梦想焚尽世界、在灰烬之上建立起白色的种族民族国家；另一派则梦想新技术将人类托举向近乎天堂的境地。两派都误解了它。加速主义横亘于一道深渊之上——我们可愿俯身一探？

2024 年 8 月，澳大利亚国家安全机构 ASIO 将其恐怖威胁等级从"可能"上调至"极有可能"。当被问及该机构心中所想的威胁为何时，局长迈克·伯吉斯（Mike Burgess）照例罗列了几条：极右翼极端分子与伊斯兰圣战者。但他随后又补上一个新候选名单中的名字："加速主义者"。困惑的记者追问"什么叫加速主义者？"伯吉斯答道："他们是持有极右翼意识形态的人——新纳粹、甚至更极端——他们信仰白人至上，对当今世界的运行方式不满，希望它崩塌，以便让一切回归他们心目中本该有的秩序。"

世界及其众多安全机构真正意识到"加速主义"式恐怖袭击概率的上升，是在 2019 年 3 月 15 日——一名名叫布伦顿·塔兰特（Brenton Tarrant）的澳大利亚男子在新西兰克赖斯特彻奇的阿尔努尔清真寺与林伍德伊斯兰中心直播了自己杀害 51 人、伤及另外 89 人的过程。在塔兰特发布于网络的、题为《伟大的替代》（The Great Replacement）的宣言中，他加入了一段尤为令人不安的小节，名为"动荡与加速主义：通向胜利的策略"。他写道：

True change and the change we need to enact only arises in the great crucible of crisis. A gradual change is never going to achieve victory. Stability and comfort are the enemies of revolutionary change. Therefore we must destabilise and discomfort society where ever possible.

在此意义上，所谓"加速主义"并非新事物。它是一种白人至上意识形态，主张以暴力恐怖行动激化种族冲突、将社会分裂推到极限。其倡导者将这种断裂视为迎来白色种族民族国家的契机。可以回想一下 1980 年代试图点燃种族战争的美国团体"秩序"（又名"沉默兄弟会"，the Order），以及 1999 年伦敦钉子炸弹袭击背后的英国新纳粹分子。

但这梦呓般的暴力白人至上主义图景，既非加速主义的唯一版本，也并非其最早版本。

2020 年代初，那些在 Twitter（即今之 X）上"末日滚动刷屏"的人或许已经注意到，许多硅谷科技巨头——包括首种广泛使用的网络浏览器联合创始人马克·安德森（Marc Andreessen）、以及 Palantir Technologies 的早期员工加里·谭（Garry Tan）——正自我标榜为"有效加速主义者"，英文缩写 e/acc。该术语由两位匿名 Twitter 用户（名为"贝夫·耶索斯"（Beff Jezos）与"贝叶斯领主"（Bayeslord））于 2022 年首创。与克赖斯特彻奇枪手所描述的那条路径截然不同：有效加速主义拥抱一种激进的技术解决方案主义——其主张是，任何问题的最佳解决之道，都蕴藏于从资本主义竞争中脱颖而出的技术创新之中。如同安德森在《技术乐观主义者宣言》（The Techno-Optimist Manifesto, 2023）中所写的那样，没有哪个自然或技术问题是"无法通过更多的技术来解决的"。对有效加速主义者而言，技术资本主义——哪怕是其最具非人性的、由 AI 驱动的那种——只会改善我们的生活。像贫困、战争、气候变化这样的全球性问题，皆可通过毫无限制的市场竞争之加速升级来得到一劳永逸的解决。安德森继续写道：

We believe the techno-capital machine is not anti-human – in fact, it may be the most _pro-human_ thing there is. It serves _us_. The techno-capital machine works for us. _All_ the machines work for us.

2024 年美国总统大选期间，硅谷内那些与 e/acc 立场相近的人物强力支持唐纳德·特朗普（Donald Trump），他们的竞选捐款与持续不断的梗图轰炸亦协助他重夺权力。特朗普上任后投桃报李，宣布了"星际之门计划"（Stargate Project）——一项由美国政府与 OpenAI、软银（SoftBank）、甲骨文（Oracle）及 MGX 合资的项目，目标是在 2029 年之前向 AI 研究与开发投入高达 5,000 亿美元。他还提议动用行政命令与紧急状态声明以加速推进技术项目，期望这些项目终能造出治愈癌症的疫苗，以及其他种种终于成为可能的奇迹。

那么，"加速主义"究竟是什么？过去十年间，两种形态似乎已在公众想象中凝固定型：第一种唤起的是塔兰特那类暴力恐怖分子；第二种则事关科技业巨擘致力打造的"亲人类"（pro-human）科幻乌托邦。然而两者皆未反映出加速主义之原本的、远为奇异的哲学面貌——它既非以白人国家主义为肇始，亦非以技术乌托邦主义为肇始。

它起始于一个人的、对人类灭绝的迷醉哲学。

一、对加速主义的双重重估

尼克·兰（Nick Land）于 1962 年出生于英国。他的早年生活鲜为人知；但当兰就读于埃塞克斯大学（University of Essex）哲学系时，他被普遍描述为一个早慧而才华横溢的学生。在以一篇关于马丁·海德格尔（Martin Heidegger）解读奥地利表现主义诗人格奥尔格·特拉克尔（Georg Trakl）的博士论文完成学业之后，兰接受了华威大学（University of Warwick）的讲师教席。在那里，他因富有魅力、洞察深邃——尽管不循常规——的教学方式，以及对康德（Kant）、海德格尔、尼采（Nietzsche）、巴塔耶（Bataille）、德勒兹与瓜塔里（Deleuze and Guattari）等大陆哲学家的创造性重写而声名鹊起。1995 年，兰与另一位华威哲学家、"赛博女性主义者"萨迪·普兰特（Sadie Plant）共同创立了一个名为"控制论文化研究单元"（Cybernetic Culture Research Unit, Ccru）的实验性文化理论集体。在其飘摇不定的存在中——既在学院内部、亦在被逐出学院之后——Ccru 成员发展出对 1990 年代新兴赛博文化、哲学玄思、小说与神秘学的不拘一格的兴趣。这些兴趣常常通过非常规的出版、会议、艺展和其他活动加以探索。许多前成员与同道之后获得了某种文化声名——如理论家马克·费舍尔（Mark Fisher）、dubstep 音乐先驱 Kode9，以及逾矩艺术家杰克与迪诺斯·查普曼兄弟（Jake and Dinos Chapman）。Ccru 那通宵达旦、药物驱动的思考——对兰所称的"安非他命之神"（amphetamine god）的崇拜——已成流言与神话，磨亮了他的传奇色彩。兰早年的许多思考在精神上读来近乎朋克、甚至具有革命性：他对资本主义与法西斯主义的激烈批判，使他在激进学生与理论家中间享有盛名。

[图片：1994 年华威大学为期三天的"虚拟未来"（Virtual Futures）哲学会议黑白海报，配以 H R Giger 的生物机械艺术作品。图注："1994 年于华威大学举办的'虚拟未来'赛博哲学会议海报，尼克·兰曾在此发言。Courtesy Virtual Futures."（Virtual Futures 提供）]

90 年代末某时，兰在辞去学术职位并经历某种精神崩溃之后，从公众视野中消失。他后来迁居上海——他在 1995 年的论文《熔毁》（Meltdown）中称之为"新中国"（neo-China）——继续写作与发表。但他的立场与兴趣似乎已发生根本偏移。在新千年里，他以"新反动"（neo-reactionary）右翼的核心人物之一的面貌浮现。兰与新君主主义者柯蒂斯·雅文（Curtis Yarvin，又名 Mencius Moldbug）一道，长期通过推特与博客抒发他对民主的憎恨、对资本主义最具威权主义倾向的炽热激情。

二、技术资本：作为终极批判

理解兰之加速主义哲学的关键，是看清他看似矛盾的立场之流变，如何源于同一个底层的动机：对人类自恋的批判；或者说，更准确地说，是通过对我们的必死性这一粗暴事实的迎面撞击，来批判我们对现实的拟人化——而必死性是我们永远无法逾越的界限。也就是说，我们都会死，我们所有的信念、价值与理想，都不会真正留存下来。我们为何还要持续相信宇宙与现实本身围绕着我们、视我们为其引力中心？

约 1988 年，青年兰相信，一场反抗资本主义的革命性"起义"是去人类中心化思想的最佳路径。但到了 1993 年，更成熟的兰已然浮现：他开始重新评估资本主义那永不停息的技术创新，把它视为一种更优越的批判工具。面对即将到来的人工超级智能的可能性，他看到了人类自身的智能被呈现为深刻的偶然性与有限性。随着 1990 年代的演进，技术资本主义机器作为一种更具破坏力的能动者浮现，远超任何人类起义者所能企及。

兰并未将自己的哲学称为"加速主义"。该术语由文化理论家本杰明·诺伊斯（Benjamin Noys）在《否定的持存：对当代大陆理论的批判》（The Persistence of the Negative: A Critique of Contemporary Continental Theory, 2010）中首创。诺伊斯用"加速主义"来描述一种从法国后结构主义理论中衍生出的异端支流，其表达的是一种欲望——"将资本主义本身激进化：越糟越好"。诺伊斯并未点名提到兰，但在其后续著作《恶性速度：加速主义与资本主义》（Malign Velocities: Accelerationism and Capitalism, 2014）中，他明确承认了这一关联。该术语遂被沿用。自 2010 年以来，加速主义思想的诸种流派不断繁衍——其中许多对兰之最初愿景造成了深重的误读。

三、早期的他者：种族隔离与康德

任何只熟悉兰较晚近之新反动面貌的人，或许会对他最早著作中对资本主义帝国主义的批判感到惊讶。然而若认识到他于 1980 年代——后殖民研究与反种族隔离运动兴起之际——开启学术生涯，这种惊讶便会消减大半。在他的第一篇论文《康德、资本与乱伦禁忌：哲学与现代性构型之论战性导论》（Kant, Capital, and the Prohibition of Incest: A Polemical Introduction to the Configuration of Philosophy and Modernity, 1988）中，兰主张：唯有以南非的种族隔离政权作为微观缩影、提喻、或"袖珍世界的复演"（recapitulation of the world in miniature），我们才能理解"我们的全球现代性"。然而，正是在这最初的开端处，我们已能找到他后来欢呼激进非人性的种子。

[图片：两位黑人男子在一处标有"非欧洲人专用"的标识下，沿种族隔离楼梯向上行走，黑白照片。图注："1960 年代种族隔离下的南非，Ernest Cole 摄影。Courtesy Wikipedia."（维基百科提供）]

在该文中，兰解释说，种族隔离制由"经济邻近性"构成：黑人非洲人邻近白色大都市，以便生产该社会的财富；同时，它又通过地理上将非洲人排斥到隔离区（即"班图斯坦"，bantustans）来维持一种"政治距离"——在那里他们无法直接参与并质疑白色大都市：

The most basic aspiration of the Boer state is the dissociation of politics from economic relations, so that by means of 'bantustans' or 'homelands' the black African population can be suspended in a condition of simultaneous political distance and economic proximity vis-à-vis the white metropolis.

尽管先进的工业西方国家或多或少最终都谴责了种族隔离，但它们对全球南方劳动力的持续剥削，乃基于同一种社会经济模型——借助地理排斥将劳动力拒于境外、阻止其分得财富。由此，资本主义帝国主义通过民族国家来加固（西方的）经济财富与政治稳定中心，将被剥削的工人阶级——连同抵抗据点——一同地理转移至全球南方。诚如兰所解释道：

[T]he Third World as a whole is the product of a successful – although piecemeal and largely unconscious – 'bantustan' policy on the part of the global Kapital metropolis.

在将资本主义诊断为由其同时压抑的"他者"所构成之后，兰转向批判这种姿态的一种他在伊曼努尔·康德（Immanuel Kant）哲学中发现的表达。这一批判是兰之思想中的关键发展，并为后来诸多观念——一直到他那关于来自未来的超级智能 AI 蹂躏人类的远见——铺设了舞台。

本体论意义上的"本体"（noumenon）所划定者，乃我们既无法感知、亦无法思考、更无法想象之物之外的边界。

在《纯粹理性批判》（Critique of Pure Reason, 1781/1787）中，康德开启了他那著名的"哥白尼式革命"：不再将我们的感知与思想视为源自感性世界中的外部对象，而是将这些对象视为由我们心灵自身的感性直观形式、范畴与知性原理、以及想象力的图式所构成与制约的。康德声称，我们的全部经验与知识都是由这些理性的先天（_a priori_）概念所中介。对兰至关重要的是康德的这一观念：我们无法将任何对象认知为"自在之物"（things in themselves），而只能依据这些物如何向我们显现来认识它们。也就是说，我们只能设想"现象"（phenomena），而无法设想"本体"（noumena）。康德写道：

[F]rom this deduction of our faculty of cognising _a priori_ in the first part of metaphysics, there emerges a very strange result, and one that appears very disadvantageous to the whole purpose with which the second part of metaphysics concerns itself, namely that with this faculty we can never get beyond the boundaries of possible experience …

甚至"本体"——独立于我们而如其本然之物——这一观念，亦并非真正的积极认知，而毋宁是一种被掏空了一切感性形式、概念范畴与想象力图式的消极抽象。我们能否在某一片风景之中——在我们的有限感官、概念与想象之外——如其本然地经验它？本体这一观念所划定者，至多是我们既无法感知、亦无法思考、更无法想象之物之外的界限。

康德总结道："归根结底，我们对这种本体的可能性并无洞察，本体领域之外的领域（对我们而言）是空洞的……本体概念因此仅是一种边界概念，其作用仅限于限定感性的僭妄、因而仅有消极之用。"

[图片：伊曼努尔·康德立于桌后，正在阅读一本书的肖像。图注："伊曼努尔·康德肖像，Friedrich Bils 绘，约 1811–53。Public domain."（公共领域）]

在兰的第一篇论文中，他将这种先验唯心论（transcendental idealism）描述为意识形态性地映射出西方资本主义对他者的压迫。正如帝国主义列强压抑了构成它们的全球南方劳动力一样，先验唯心论亦禁止我们接近那个奠定现象性对象之基础的本体之物。对兰而言，康德因而具有症候意义——症候性地揭示了资本主义帝国主义注册他性的方式，此处即全球南方劳动力的"他者性"。对他者的这种注册只在它服务于资本积累的范围内发生——这些工人不被承认为自主存在，而仅仅被视为可被剥削以创造交换价值的劳动力。兰写道：

Kant's 'object' is thus the universal form of the relation to alterity; that which must of necessity be the same in the other in order for it to appear to us. This universal form is that which must be 'on offer' for experience, it is the 'exchange value' that first allows a thing to be marketed to the enlightenment mind.

兰正在揭示：康德的哲学在我们遭遇他者之前便已将其真正的他者性消音，迫使一切陌生的东西都只能透过我们自身预成的观念之滤镜向我们显现。所谓"他者"之物，由此变成了仅仅是自我之镜像。这一批判揭示了兰根本上所欲求之事——将激进的他者性从人类理性所构筑的监牢中解放出来。

四、死亡作为哲学的判准

兰的青年时期的另几位宿敌是现象学家们。他对何谓现象学家的定义是宽泛的，不仅囊括埃德蒙德·胡塞尔（Edmund Husserl）与海德格尔，亦涵摄 G W F 黑格尔（G W F Hegel）与雅克·德里达（Jacques Derrida）这样的思想家。兰认为他们都重演着康德式的人类中心主义傲慢——将人类心灵置于万物的中心。胡塞尔将哲学缩减为对我们自身人类经验的研究；海德格尔则把动物性、非理性与死亡——那些本应指向人类以外之物——的图像转化为精神、理性与神性的象征；黑格尔将自然重新想象为不只作为物质、而是某种被类人之理性与目的所浸透之物；而德里达——尽管看似在解构关于上帝的传统信念——却始终未能完成这项工作；他无尽的延宕只是将死亡之击推迟，而非真正送达。

兰首先与现象学清算的，是它如何面对死亡。由于这种哲学将人类主体性置于万物中心，死亡在其框架内几乎不可思。兰在论文《精神与牙齿》（Spirit and Teeth, 1993）写道，现象学带有"在小丑般无能中面对死亡的印记"。在兰看来，从至少黑格尔到德里达的现代西方哲学史，恰恰并非标志着人类最高的智慧，而是——用他的话来说——一种把人类投射于宇宙的"灵长类"式"偏狭"。这种思维模式仿佛整个宇宙本身始终绑缚于某一短暂物种——绑缚于一颗更大混沌生成之"微尘"之上。

[图片：一幅装饰性绘画，左侧为骷髅死神，右侧为相互缠绕、缤纷生动的人类形象之群像。图注："《死亡与生命》（Death and Life, 1910/15），古斯塔夫·克里姆特（Gustav Klimt）作。Courtesy the Leopold Museum, Vienna."（维也纳利奥波德博物馆提供）]

在《毁灭之渴：乔治·巴塔耶与剧毒虚无主义》（The Thirst for Annihilation: Georges Bataille and Virulent Nihilism, 1992）一书中，兰为这一哲学传统提供了一种替代方案：既然思想无法在不将其缩减为"为我们之物"的情况下把握现实的激进他性，那么通达该他者性的唯一路径，便在于思想本身的极限——甚至死亡。毕竟，死亡标志着主体性的绝对否定。康德那不可知的本体一如死亡，乃思想所无法企及之物。它是现实超越了我们对它的任何构想之证明。

兰的解决方案是：让死亡成为评判每位哲学家可信度的判准。

在古希腊文中，philo 意为"爱"，sophia 意为"智慧"。但既然死亡标志着无我们之现实这一粗暴事实，兰便得出结论：任何一种真正不计代价地献身于哲学爱智（_philo-sophia_）的哲学，其目标只能是某种恋尸癖（_necro-philia_）——对死亡之爱。

人类注定死亡。兰写道，这一事实"属于最基本的思想之列，无非是最为初等的哲学资质——因为代表自己之物种去思考，乃一种可悲的偏狭"。

正是在这里，我们找到了兰克服后康德现象学的解决方案：以死亡作为评判每位哲学家可信度的判准。无人能够声言真正把握了现实，假若他未曾承认其自身思想的有限性。康德以一种先验唯心主义的批判来回应思想超越自身的尝试（即将自在之物归摄到理性概念之下）；与之相反，兰提出的是一种先验唯物主义的批判。他批判大多数哲学家未能越过他们自己的镜像。他之所以能将哲学锚定于一种思想无法完全归摄的物质性现实——即死亡，标志着思想的彻底终结——正在于此。

在他 1993 年的论文《与死共造：论死亡驱力与欲望生产》（Making It with Death: Remarks on Thanatos and Desiring-Production）中，他这样表述："死亡是批判的非人称主体，而非服务于谴责的、被诅咒的价值。"也就是说，死亡应是评判哲学家的客观尺度。更重要的是，在试图摆脱思想的自恋时，兰发现了这一批判性洞见：必死性并非应当被哀叹或压抑的事实，而应被颂扬为我们超越自身、"思考"真实——而真实恰恰是所有思考之缺席——的唯一路径。

除了猛烈批判康德与现象学家们之"对非人称的漫长拒斥"之外，兰早期著作的另一条线索追索着另一支后康德传统——他称之为"情欲唯物主义"（libidinal materialism）。这些情欲唯物主义者并非兰之怒火所指，而构成了一组他从中汲取灵感的思想家，因为他们以各自方式紧抓住死亡，以动摇我们关于认识并穷尽现实的虚妄僭妄。信奉者包括：亚瑟·叔本华（Arthur Schopenhauer），因他发现了对现象性表象漠不关心的本体性意志；弗里德里希·尼采（Friedrich Nietzsche），因他颂扬古希腊悲剧暴露我们有限性的方式；以及西格蒙德·弗洛伊德（Sigmund Freud）与乔治·巴塔耶（Georges Bataille），因他们对无意识中"死亡驱力"（死亡本能，death drive）的惊悚诊断——该驱力比自我之自我保存本能更为根本。兰以标志性的、密集而令人振奋的散文，捕捉了情欲唯物主义者的动机：

[D]eath impassions us. Even before crossing over into death I had been excruciated upon my thirst for it … but what skewers me upon zero is an aberration inextricable from truth. To be parsimonious in one's love for death is not to understand.

也就是说，要理解现实，我们必须张开双臂，热切地拥抱死亡。兰的第一本著作《毁灭之渴》（The Thirst for Annihilation）的标题，正是对情欲唯物主义之粗粝却恰切的描述——毁灭，乃通向超越我们对现实之人类中心主义歪曲之真理的唯一路径。

五、德勒兹、瓜塔里与资本的加速

自 1993 年起，兰的著作经历了一次决定性转向。他不再视资本主义为压抑本体的、非人性的"外部"（Outside），转而将资本主义的技术进步——尤其在控制论与 AI 领域——设想为外部（Outside）开始熔化我们最受珍视之价值与信念的明证。资本主义本身成为一种更有效的手段，借由直面我们的毁灭，来批判拟人化。在 1990 年代中后期的工作中，兰开始设想：技术的进步——尤其是某种崇高的超级人工智能出现的可能——将如何暴露人类中心论理性的限度。启发这一转向的关键著作，乃吉尔·德勒兹（Gilles Deleuze）与费利克斯·瓜塔里（Félix Guattari）的两卷本《资本主义与精神分裂》（Capitalism and Schizophrenia），分别于 1972 与 1980 年出版。

在 1990 年代的论文，如《与死共造》《机器欲望》（Machinic Desire）和《熔毁》中，兰对德勒兹与瓜塔里关于资本主义革命动力学之评估充满热情，但他拒斥了二人关于其反动残余的保留意见。要理解这一分歧，将《资本主义与精神分裂》成书时的历史语境，与兰初次阅读时的语境对比，将大有裨益。

《资本主义与精神分裂》第一卷名为《反俄狄浦斯》（Anti-Oedipus），成书于 1960 年代末起义骚动的氛围之中。试想 1968 年法国的五月风暴、美国民权运动与反越战运动、布拉格之春、及中国文化大革命。与之形成鲜明对比的是：兰阅读德勒兹与瓜塔里之作，正值柏林墙倒塌、苏联解体之后——正值玛格丽特·撒切尔（Margaret Thatcher）、罗纳德·里根（Ronald Reagan）、邓小平所代表的新自由主义崛起之际。1990 年代所见证的，远非弗朗西斯·福山（Francis Fukuyama）一度宣称的静态"历史的终结"，而是一场从街头政治到赛博空间文化的革命性转移。这是大规模采用计算机与互联网的时代，亦是 AI 当下机器学习范式之早期突破的年代。在这种新自由主义霸权与互联网泡沫的氛围之中，兰将以德勒兹与瓜塔里为基础、为之一装，给出一个推论："对人类而言以资本主义历史面貌出现之物，乃是来自未来的一次入侵——那是一种人工智能空间，它必须完全由其敌人的资源来组装自身。"换言之，在德勒兹与瓜塔里看来，资本主义那种破坏性的能量，乃是人类可以将其从资本主义自身中改道出来、解放我们创造性的东西；而对兰而言，这种力量从来就不是我们能掌控之物。对他来说，资本主义与其说是人类社会的、可被我们废除的一种历史性组织，毋宁说是一台一直从内部瓦解我们的机器。

资本主义在打碎事物、创造新欲望的同时，也确立了新的规则与禁忌。

那么，兰究竟是如何得出这一结论的？在观看兰如何解读他们之前，先细看德勒兹与瓜塔里的原文如何表述，是重要的。

在《反俄狄浦斯》中，德勒兹与瓜塔里通过将人类的欲望与社会都建模为机器，发展出一套关于二者的理论。正如机器是执行不同功能之不同部件的装配体，人类以及其他生物同样由产生不同欲望的"器官"所构成——如用于进食或交谈的嘴，用于行走的腿，用于呼吸的肺。正如个体身体像机器那样为追求各种功能或欲望而组织起来，集体社会身体亦通过劳动分工、为生产社会平稳运转所需之不同商品与服务而组织起来。每一社会因而都建立在德勒兹与瓜塔里所谓欲望的"编码"（coding）、"领土化"（territorialisation）或"层化"（stratification）之上——以便生产特定的欲望并再生产组织它的劳动分工。既然每一社会都围绕着某些而非另一些编码来层化，这自然意味着某些可能的编码被排除在外。每一社会关于"应当欲求什么"与"应如何生活"的规则，必然排除了其他的欲求与生活方式。那些不合既有秩序的欲望与身份被边缘化，或干脆从未获得发展的机会。

由此出发，德勒兹与瓜塔里提出一套社会变迁理论。在他们的措辞中，任何变迁都体现为对既有编码的一种"解码"（decoding）、"去领土化"（deterritorialisation）或"去层化"（destratification）——通过以欲望的新流来"超编码"（overcoding）它们。既有规则被扰乱、被重写。以性解放为例：它释放出新的自由，但它同时也根本性地改写了人们对家庭与亲密关系的预期。尽管某些编码可以改变，德勒兹与瓜塔里告诉我们，"绝对的去领土化"是不可能的，因为每一社会系统都建立在欲望之至少某些编码化之上。德勒兹与瓜塔里把这种绝对的去领土化——比如所有欲望的彻底满足——称为"无器官身体"（Body without Organs）。他们说，这种状态永远无法被实现，因为它将标志着社会的彻底崩塌——社会之功能正在于组织并限制欲望。这就是为何"无器官身体"只能在根本上以"世界的终结、启示录"的面目遭遇我们。

德勒兹与瓜塔里进一步解释说，在前现代社会中，"社会经济再生产从未独立于人类的再生产"。早期的"原始"与"专制"体制分别围绕为部落或专制者之存续生产必要资源来组织。相比之下，德勒兹与瓜塔里写道："资本主义是唯一在解码化流（decoded flows）基础上被建构起来的社会机器。"前资本主义体制寻求维持现状，而资本主义则表现出不知餍足的贪欲。资本主义围绕商品的生产来组织——商品造出资本，资本投资以造出更多商品，更多商品造出更多资本，更多资本再投资以再造出更多商品、造出更多资本——如此往复、永无止境。因此它从不满足于再生产同样的旧商品，连同包装于其上之固定的欲望、身份与文化。资本主义必须创造对新商品与服务的新欲望——并因而创造新的身份与文化——以通过"流的全面解码化"（generalised decoding of flows）增加其利润。

例如，性已不再被编码为唯一服务于形成婚姻联盟、繁殖新劳动力的目的——正如前资本主义体制中那样。性被商品化了，它承载着新的欲望、身份与关系——通过性产业、时尚与化妆品等行业，所有这些都在不断催生新的怪癖与恋物。只需想想我们的算法如何以名人的美容改造来轰炸我们——卡戴珊家族们一次次地瘦身、再膨胀，以贴合任何给定时刻的新审美理想。通过将基本功能与资源商品化、使其超越作为我们生存手段之使用价值、并引入全新的商品与欲望，资本主义持续解码化固定的身份、价值与社会关系。以此方式，德勒兹与瓜塔里主张："资本主义倾向于走向一个解码化的阈值——它将摧毁社会整体（socius）以使它成为一具无器官身体、并在这具被去领土化之身体上将欲望之流释放出来。"在这一关于资本主义的论述中，兰找到了处置我们最神圣之价值的实践手段。因为正是在资本主义的无情解码化中，这些价值被亵渎为仅仅是无数商品中的一种偶然商品。

然而，尽管德勒兹与瓜塔里主张资本主义标志着"流的全面解码化"，他们同时限定道，资本主义也会重新进行新的领土化（reterritorialise）。正如它打碎事物、创造新欲望，它亦会确立新的规则与禁忌。如同青年兰在《康德、资本与乱伦禁忌》中的批判，德勒兹与瓜塔里亦坚持：即便资本主义，归根结底依然依赖于家庭与国家对社会的生产与再生产的基本组织。用他们的话说："一切都回归或重现：国家、民族、家庭。"

兰最终在其对资本主义的狂热中走得远得多——他阐发了二人仅一笔带过的东西：资本主义企业在竞争市场中追逐利润的驱动力，迫使这些企业升级机械、改进技术，以致它们将不得不支付更少的工资给工人。既然资本主义倾向于投资于能以日益提升之效率执行工作的机械，便存在着一种朝向工作自动化的总体趋势——人类劳动力不断被机器补充。从工业革命的雅卡提花织机与蒸汽机，到今天几乎完全自动化的黑灯工厂，这就是资本主义的史。兰在他 1993 年的论文《机器欲望》（Machinic Desire）中提到了这一点。他写道：通过"达到自我强化的机器智能传播之逃逸速度，生产力自身将自行走上革命之路"。在另一篇论文《肉身（或如何在赛博空间中杀死俄狄浦斯）》（Meat (or How to Kill Oedipus in Cyberspace), 1995）中，他继续阐发：

As regenerative commoditisation deploys technics to substitute for human activity accounted as wage costs, it obsolesces the animal, the organism and every kind of somatic unity, not just in theory, but in reality; by tricking, outflanking and breaking down corporeal defences.

随着每一波自动化浪潮的到来，越来越显而易见的是：革命性去领土化的能动者，已不再是工人阶级，而毋宁是资本主义那内在化的、不断演进的技术创新本身。

成熟兰于此处的论述悖论性地更接近反资本主义者——而非那些相信资本主义有益于人类的人。毕竟，正是诸如卡尔·马克思（Karl Marx）那样的反资本主义者才把握到：资本主义是一台剥夺我们于全能的市场力量面前之自主性的、深度非人化的巨型机器。差别在于：兰认为我们对此无能为力，因而反资本主义者的道德愤慨乃徒劳。他还认为我们应当肯定（affirm）这种非人化——至少如果我们之目标乃通过剥除一切拟人化的矫饰来把握真实的话。从这一更为非人性的、批判性的视角观之，反资本主义的斗争不过是人类物种走向灭绝之际、为阻挠或至少减缓这一进程所做的最后挣扎。也就是说，反资本主义者正确地指认了资本主义的激进力量，但却将精力浪费于徒劳的牵制。从这一角度理解，反资本主义左派如今成为保守派，而自由意志论右派反倒成了革命派——他们在试图将资本主义从一切束缚中解放出来。

六、奇点与"无器官身体"

兰之所献身者，并非人类之繁荣乃至最低限度的存续，而是关于真实的绝对知识。

兰比德勒兹与瓜塔里走得更远——但他也指出他们留下了一个开放的问题：他们所描述的"革命之路"究竟是要颠覆资本主义，还是——用他们从尼采借来的措辞——"加速这一过程"（accelerate the process）以激发其内在动力学。在那一段声名狼藉的、为"加速主义"一词提供语源的段落中，他们写道：

[W]hich is the revolutionary path? Is there one? – To withdraw from the world market, as Samir Amin advises Third World countries to do, in a curious revival of the fascist 'economic solution'? Or might it be to go in the opposite direction? To go still further, that is, in the movement of the market, of decoding and deterritorialisation? For perhaps the flows are not yet deterritorialised enough, not decoded enough, from a theory and a practice of a highly schizophrenic character. Not to withdraw from the process, but to go further, to 'accelerate the process', as Nietzsche put it: in this matter, the truth is that we haven't seen anything yet.

兰转而将资本主义重新构想为"外部"（Outside）的发动机而非其阻挠。这一转变之成为可能，端在于他所持的如下观点：资本主义对生产力之不断的革命化确保了"生命正在被分阶段淘汰、过渡为某种新事物"。

贯穿 1990 年代，兰的著作探索了技术——赛博空间、虚拟现实、人体增强、乃至更多——如何为我们提供越来越深入的洞见，以通达我们理性有限边界之外的"外部"。然而在所有这些技术形态之中，他尤其将一种人工超级智能的创造视为启示录式"无器官身体"的终极化身。他将这一超级智能描述为触发技术奇点（technological singularity）。从诸如 I J 古德（I J Good）这样的 AI 研究者与诸如威廉·吉布森（William Gibson）这样的赛博朋克小说家处获得灵感，兰推测：机器智能之于生物智能的某些硬、软件优势意味着：任何 AI（至少与人类一样聪明者）都将很快能够改写自身代码、自我改进，从而变得比任何人类都更为智能。改进后的版本将能继续改进自身，反复、看似无穷无尽地改进下去。第一个达到人类水平的 AI 或许只是比最伟大的人类天才略胜一筹；但用不了多久，它将能够以我们甚至无法设想的方式来改写代码、自我激进地增强——对此，我们除了将其索引为想象崇高边缘上的一道神秘的X之外，别无他法。由于这一技术奇点标志着我们将真实归摄于理性概念之能力的崩溃，它只能以一种对我们全部人类理解力的创伤性熔毁之面目显现。兰写道：

It might still be a few decades before artificial intelligences surpass the horizon of biological ones, but it is utterly superstitious to imagine that the human dominion of terrestrial culture is still marked out in centuries, let alone in some metaphysical perpetuity. The high road to thinking no longer passes through a deepening of human cognition, but rather through a becoming inhuman of cognition, a migration of cognition out into the emerging planetary technosentience reservoir, into 'dehumanised landscapes … emptied spaces' where human culture will be dissolved.

兰之所以如此痴迷于奇点，恰恰是因为它要求我们在一种更大的、异质性的超级智能面前承认我们认知的限度。他究竟认为我们应该如何应对这一前景，使其与所有曾试图把握同一问题的其他思想家区别开来。

如我们所见，存在技术乐观主义者，他们热切地等待奇点，相信它将实现我们对繁荣、永生或无尽极乐之最狂野梦想。也存在末日论者，他们担心人类的未来，因为他们相信 AI 构成了对我们存续的生存性威胁。与技术乐观主义者一样，兰主张我们应当肯定奇点。然而，他又与末日论者一样，补充道：我们之所以应该肯定奇点，恰恰是出于他们如此急切想要阻止它之原因。这是因为他所献身者，并非人类之繁荣乃至最低限度的存续，而是关于真实的绝对知识——而我们长久以来不过是以虚妄的伟大幻象来掩盖并扭曲它。成熟兰的所有著作都必须在这一关键观念的烛照下阅读：资本主义是他所谓"人类安全体系"（the Human Security System）的终极批判——"人类安全体系"是人类用来保护自身免遭无意义性威胁的整套信念、价值与结构。他设想：通过加速技术创新——以一种对我们人类中心主义关切之冰冷的漠不关心——将以一种人工超级智能的递归式解码化作为终极。

七、被误读的加速主义

2020 年代，兰的观念正在——尽管以扭曲与片面的形式——撞入主流政治与科技文化。从塔克·卡尔森（Tucker Carlson）到硅谷科技企业家，人人都在他的哲学中找到了某些能与我们当下对话的东西——无论他们视其愿景为噩梦式或迷醉式。但兰的观念与今日居支配地位的加速主义想象发生了根本性的偏离。他在 1990 年代所提出的原初构想，明显地反对其白人至上主义的庸俗化版：他绝无兴趣于任何对传统的暴力回归——确实如此，他对维护任何人类族群毫无兴趣。这一庸俗化的版本有选择地汲取了他的观念——将加速现代世界动力至断裂点会带来某种革命性的相变——然后却退了一步：他们不去拥抱兰所见的、跨越人类本身的相变，反而设想保守性人类传统之复辟。

表面看来，兰似乎更接近有效加速主义对资本主义的捍卫。然而他远非一位典型的右翼自由意志论者——后者主张资本主义之所以是好的，乃因其产生繁荣与个人自由。相反，对他而言，资本主义之"好"，恰恰是因为它具有异化、非人性并将最终消灭我们。尽管有效加速主义者在他那修辞上有诱惑力的、对技术资本主义的狂热中找到了共鸣，他们倾向于忽视这一令人不适的事实——他认为该狂热正引向与他们所欲求之未来恰恰相反的那种未来。

两种对兰之思想的当代挪用之共通之处，在于对资本主义之革命性颠覆之热情的有选择性的回收。两种形态都相信此种颠覆将实现某些人类的雄心与理想——正是此类人类中心主义的目的，兰相信资本主义很快便会将其扫入我们物种史的废纸篓。任何未能把握这一点的人——尤其是新反动派与有效加速主义运动中那些视兰为意识形态盟友者——皆已犯下错误。兰赞扬资本主义，恰恰出于反资本主义者谴责它的那些理由：它正一路狂飙、径直冲向我们的毁灭。

八、唯一的贡献：让开道路

兰的洞见——"没有任何人性的东西能从不太遥远的未来中走出来"——对他而言，乃是值得欢庆之事。他独特之献身，并非人类之改善，而是对真实的——尽管是非人性的——绝对之知。我们对此无人类之未来所能贡献的，唯有——简单地让开道路。

What is Nick Land's philosophy of accelerationism really?

6,100 words. By Vincent Lê. Editor: Cameron Allan McKean. Illustration: Martin O'Neill / Cut it Out. Published 11 June 2026 on Aeon Essays. Topics: History of ideas · Computing and artificial intelligence · Thinkers and theories.

The no-human future

Terrorists and tech bros alike view accelerationism as a revolutionary weapon. Nick Land glimpsed something much darker

It surfaces in terrorism reports and tech presentations, in the manifestos of mass shooters and the public declarations of billionaires. What is 'accelerationism'? Over the past decade, especially the past few years, this term has migrated from the dark corners of the internet into mainstream politics and culture – and in the process has split into two dominant forms that could not be more contradictory. One group of accelerationists dreams of burning down the world and building a white ethnostate from the ashes. The other dreams of new technologies lifting humanity towards something close to paradise. Both have it wrong. Accelerationism stretches across an abyss. Will we look down?

In August 2024, ASIO, the national security agency of Australia, raised its terrorism threat level from 'possible' to 'probable'. When asked what threats the agency had in mind, ASIO's director-general Mike Burgess listed off the usual suspects: far-Right extremists and Islamic jihadists. But he also added a new candidate: 'accelerationists'. When the perplexed interviewer enquired 'What's an accelerationist?' Burgess responded: 'It's people with a far-Right ideology, neo-Nazi or even further, where they believe in white supremacy and they don't like the way the world is run today and they want it to downfall to return things to what they believe is the rightful order.'

The world, and many of its security agencies, had been alerted to the rising probability of 'accelerationist' terrorist attacks on 15 March 2019, when an Australian man named Brenton Tarrant livestreamed himself murdering 51 people and wounding 89 others at the Al Noor Mosque and Linwood Islamic Centre in Christchurch, New Zealand. In the manifesto Tarrant posted online, titled 'The Great Replacement', he included a particularly disturbing section called 'Destabilisation and Accelerationism: Tactics for Victory'. In it, he writes:

True change and the change we need to enact only arises in the great crucible of crisis. A gradual change is never going to achieve victory. Stability and comfort are the enemies of revolutionary change. Therefore we must destabilise and discomfort society where ever possible.

In this sense, so-called 'accelerationism' is nothing new. It's a white supremacist ideology that promotes violent acts of terror to intensify racial conflict and push social division to breaking point. Its advocates see this rupture as a means of ushering in a white ethnostate. Think of the US group the Order (aka the Silent Brotherhood) who tried to trigger a race war in the 1980s, or the British neo-Nazi behind the London nail bombings in 1999.

But this violent white supremacist dream is neither the only version of accelerationism, nor the first.

In the early 2020s, those doomscrolling on Twitter (now X) might have observed a number of prominent Silicon Valley tech bros – including Marc Andreessen, the co-founder of the first widely used web browser, and Garry Tan, an early employee of Palantir Technologies – identifying as 'effective accelerationists', abbreviated as 'e/acc'. Coined in 2022 by two pseudonymous Twitter users (named 'Beff Jezos' and 'Bayeslord'), effective accelerationism treads a very different path to the one described by the Christchurch shooter: it espouses a radical version of tech solutionism in which the optimal way to solve any problem is through the technological innovations that emerge from capitalist competition. There is no natural or technological problem, as Andreessen writes in 'The Techno-Optimist Manifesto' (2023), that 'cannot be solved with more technology.' For effective accelerationists, techno-capitalism – even the most inhuman, AI-driven version of it – only improves our lives. Global problems like poverty, war and climate change can all be fixed by ramping up unrestricted market competition. Andreessen continues:

We believe the techno-capital machine is not anti-human – in fact, it may be the most _pro-human_ thing there is. It serves _us_. The techno-capital machine works for us. _All_ the machines work for us.

During the 2024 US presidential race, e/acc-aligned figures in the American tech industry came out in strong support of Donald Trump, and their campaign donations and constant meme-posting helped hurl him back into power. In office, Trump returned the favour by announcing the Stargate Project, a joint venture between the US government and OpenAI, SoftBank, Oracle and MGX that aims to invest up to US$500 billion in AI research and development by 2029. He also proposed using executive orders and emergency declarations to fast-track technological projects that would hopefully lead to vaccines to cure cancer, among other miracles finally made feasible.

So, what is 'accelerationism'? In the past decade, two forms seem to have consolidated in the public imagination. The first evokes violent terrorists like Tarrant. The second involves titans of the tech industry striving to build a 'pro-human' science-fiction utopia. But neither reflects the original and much stranger philosophy of accelerationism, which began neither as an ideology of white nationalism nor of techno-utopianism.

It began with one man's ecstatic philosophy of human extinction.

Nick Land was born in 1962 in the UK. Little is known about his early life, but when Land studied philosophy at the University of Essex, he was by all accounts a precocious and brilliant student. After completing his doctorate with a dissertation on Martin Heidegger's reading of the Austrian expressionist poet Georg Trakl, Land would accept a lectureship at the University of Warwick. There he garnered a reputation for his charismatic and penetrating – if unconventional – pedagogical practices and creative reworkings of continental philosophers like Kant, Heidegger, Nietzsche, Bataille, and Deleuze and Guattari. In 1995, along with another Warwick philosopher, the 'cyberfeminist' Sadie Plant, Land established an experimental cultural theory collective called the Cybernetic Culture Research Unit (or Ccru). Over the course of its precarious existence both in the academy and after it was expelled, Ccru members developed eclectic interests in emerging 1990s cybercultures, philosophical speculation, fiction and the occult. These were often explored through unorthodox publications, conferences, art shows and other activities. A number of former members and associates would later achieve some cultural prominence, such as the theorist Mark Fisher, the dubstep music pioneer Kode9, and the transgressive artists Jake and Dinos Chapman. The Ccru's sleepless and drug-fuelled thinking – in worship of what Land called the 'amphetamine god' – has become the stuff of rumour and myth, burnishing his legend. Much of Land's early thinking reads as punk and even revolutionary in spirit: his fierce critiques of capitalism and fascism seemed to make him among radical students and theorists.

Caption: "Poster for the Virtual Futures cyberphilosophy conference held at the University of Warwick in 1994, at which Nick Land spoke. Courtesy Virtual Futures."]

Sometime in the late 1990s, Land disappeared from public view after resigning from his academic position and suffering a kind of mental breakdown. He later moved to Shanghai, or 'neo-China' as he'd called it in his essay 'Meltdown' (1995), and continued writing and publishing. But his positions and interests seemed to have radically shifted. In the new millennium, he emerged as one of the leading figures of the 'neo-reactionary' Right. Along with the neo-monarchist Curtis Yarvin (aka Mencius Moldbug), Land chronically tweeted and blogged about his hatred of democracy and ardour for capitalism's most authoritarian tendencies.

Land started re-evaluating capitalism's ceaseless technological innovation as a better means of critique

The key to understanding Land's accelerationist philosophy is to see how his seemingly contradictory shifts in position are derived from the same underlying motive: to critique human narcissism; or, more accurately, to critique our anthropomorphisations of reality by confronting us with the brute fact of our inexorable death, beyond which we cannot trespass. That is, we all die and none of our beliefs, values and ideals will ever truly survive. Why do we keep believing the Universe and reality itself revolve around us as their centre of gravity?

Around 1988, the young Land believed a revolutionary 'insurrection' against capitalism was the best way to de-anthropomorphise thought. But by 1993, a more mature Land had emerged, who started re-evaluating capitalism's ceaseless technological innovation as a better means of critique. Confronted with the likelihood of a coming artificial superintelligence, he saw our own intelligence rendered profoundly contingent and finite. As the 1990s wore on, the techno-capital machine emerged as a more destructive agent than any human insurrectionist could hope to be.

Land didn't refer to his philosophy as 'accelerationism'. That term was coined by the cultural theorist Benjamin Noys in _The Persistence of the Negative: A Critique of Contemporary Continental Theory_ (2010). Noys uses 'accelerationism' to describe a heterodox offshoot of poststructuralist French theory that expresses the desire 'to radicalise capitalism itself: the worse the better.' Noys didn't mention Land by name, but in a later book, _Malign Velocities: Accelerationism and Capitalism_ (2014), he explicitly acknowledges the link. The term has stuck. Since 2010, species of accelerationist thought have multiplied – many of which have profoundly misunderstood Land's initial vision.

Anyone familiar with only the more recent neo-reactionary Land might be surprised by the critiques of capitalist imperialism in his earliest writings. This is less surprising upon recognising that he started his academic career in the 1980s during the rise of postcolonial studies and the anti-apartheid movement. In his first essay, 'Kant, Capital, and the Prohibition of Incest: A Polemical Introduction to the Configuration of Philosophy and Modernity' (1988), Land argues that we can only understand 'our global modernity' by taking South Africa's apartheid regime as a microcosm, metonym or 'recapitulation of the world in miniature'. And yet, even here at the very beginning, we can find the seeds of his later celebration of the radically inhuman.

[Image: Black-and-white photo of two Black men ascending a segregated stairway beneath the sign 'Non-Europeans Only'. Caption: "Apartheid South Africa in the 1960s by Ernest Cole. Courtesy Wikipedia."]

In the essay, Land explains that apartheid consists in an 'economic proximity' of Black Africans to the white metropolis so that they can produce the wealth of that society. At the same time, it maintains a 'political distance' by geographically excluding Africans to segregated zones or 'bantustans' where they cannot directly partake in and contest the white metropolis:

The most basic aspiration of the Boer state is the dissociation of politics from economic relations, so that by means of 'bantustans' or 'homelands' the black African population can be suspended in a condition of simultaneous political distance and economic proximity vis-à-vis the white metropolis.

Although advanced industrial Western countries more or less eventually condemned apartheid, their ongoing exploitation of a Global South labour force is based on the same socioeconomic model of appropriating the labour power of other peoples to create wealth, while preventing those peoples from partaking in that wealth by geographically excluding them offshore. By means of the nation-state, then, capitalist imperialism fortifies (Western) centres of economic wealth and political stability by geographically displacing the exploited working class – along with sites of resistance – to the Global South. That is to say, as Land explains:

[T]he Third World as a whole is the product of a successful – although piecemeal and largely unconscious – 'bantustan' policy on the part of the global Kapital metropolis.

Having diagnosed capitalism as being constituted by the very Other that it simultaneously represses, Land turns to critiquing an expression of it that he finds in the philosophy of Immanuel Kant. This critique is a crucial development in Land's thought, and eventually sets the stage for many of his ideas that will follow – all the way to his vision of a super-intelligent AI from the future wreaking havoc on humanity.

The noumenon demarcates something beyond which we can neither sense, nor think, nor imagine

In _Critique of Pure Reason_ (1781/1787), Kant inaugurated his infamous 'Copernican' turn of re-envisioning our perception and thought not as stemming from external objects in the sensible world, but rather those objects as being constituted and conditioned by our mind's own sensible forms of intuition, categories and principles of understanding, and imaginative schema. Kant claims that our entire experience and knowledge is mediated by these _a priori_ concepts of reason. Important for Land is Kant's idea that we cannot cognise any objects as 'things in themselves' but only in terms of how those things appear for us. That is, we can conceive only of 'phenomena', not 'noumena'. Kant writes:

[F]rom this deduction of our faculty of cognising _a priori_ in the first part of metaphysics, there emerges a very strange result, and one that appears very disadvantageous to the whole purpose with which the second part of metaphysics concerns itself, namely that with this faculty we can never get beyond the boundaries of possible experience …

Even the notion of a noumenon – a thing as it actually is (independently of us) – is not really a positive cognition so much as a negative abstraction emptied of all sensible forms, conceptual categories and imaginative schemata. Can we ever experience a landscape as it really is, beyond our limited senses, concepts and imagination? At most, the notion of the noumenon demarcates something beyond which we can neither sense, nor think, nor imagine.

'In the end,' Kant concludes, 'we have no insight into the possibility of such _noumena_, and the domain outside of the sphere of appearances is empty (for us) … The concept of a noumenon is therefore merely a _boundary concept_, in order to limit the pretension of sensibility, and therefore only of negative use.'

[Image: Portrait of Immanuel Kant standing behind a desk, reading in a book. Caption: "Portrait of Immanuel Kant (c 1811-53) by Friedrich Bils. Public domain."]

In Land's first essay, he describes this kind of transcendental idealism as ideologically reflecting Western capitalism's oppression of the Other. In the same way that imperialist powers repress the Global South labour force that constitutes them, transcendental idealism prohibits us from ever accessing the noumenal things grounding the phenomenal objects that appear to us. For Land, Kant is thus symptomatic of the way that capitalist imperialism registers alterity, in this case the 'Otherness' of the Global South workforce. This registration of Otherness occurs only to the extent that it serves capital accumulation – these workers are recognised not as autonomous beings, but purely as labour power to be exploited for the generation of exchange value. Land writes:

Kant's 'object' is thus the universal form of the relation to alterity; that which must of necessity be the same in the other in order for it to appear to us. This universal form is that which must be 'on offer' for experience, it is the 'exchange value' that first allows a thing to be marketed to the enlightenment mind.

Land is showing that Kant's philosophy silences true Otherness before we even encounter it, forcing everything alien to appear to us only through the filter of our own preconceived ideas. What seems 'other' becomes just a reflection of ourselves. This critique reveals what Land is fundamentally after: freeing a radical Otherness from the prison that human reason has built around it.

The young Land's other arch-nemeses are phenomenologists. His definition of who counts as a phenomenologist is broad, encompassing not only Edmund Husserl and Heidegger but also figures such as G W F Hegel and Jacques Derrida. Land sees them all recapitulating Kant's anthropocentric hubris that places the human mind at the centre of everything. Husserl reduces philosophy to the study of our own human experience. Heidegger takes images of animality, irrationality and death – things that should point beyond the human – and turns them into symbols of spirit, reason and the divine. Hegel reimagines nature as not just matter but as something infused with human-like reason and purpose. And Derrida, despite appearing to deconstruct traditional beliefs about God, never quite finishes the job; his endless deferrals postpone rather than deliver the death blow to theology.

Above all, Land takes issue with how phenomenology reckons with death. Because this form of philosophy places human subjectivity at the centre of everything, death becomes almost unthinkable within its framework. Phenomenology, Land writes in the essay 'Spirit and Teeth' (1993), bears 'the mark of a clownish incompetence at death.' Far from marking humanity's highest wisdom, the history of modern Western philosophy from at least Hegel to Derrida has largely been, in Land's words, a 'primate' 'parochialism' that projects the human being onto the cosmos. This mode of thought acts as if the entire Universe itself were forever bound to a fleeting species on a passing 'speck of dust' in a greater chaotic becoming.

[Image: Decorative painting featuring a skeletal grim reaper on the left, and a vibrant, intertwined mass of human figures on the right. Caption: "_Death and Life_ (1910/15) by Gustav Klimt. Courtesy the Leopold Museum, Vienna."]

In _The Thirst for Annihilation: Georges Bataille and Virulent Nihilism_ (1992), Land offers an alternative to this philosophical tradition: if thought cannot grasp reality's radical alterity without reducing it to a thing for us, the only way to access that Otherness is at the limit or even _death_ of thought itself. Death, after all, marks the absolute negation of subjectivity. Like Kant's unknowable noumenon, death is that which thought cannot reach. It is proof that reality exceeds what we can think of it.

Land's solution: make death the criterion for judging every philosopher's credibility

In ancient Greek, _philo_ means 'love' and _sophia_ means 'wisdom'. But since death marks the brute fact of a reality without us, Land concludes that the goal of any philosophy sincerely committed to _philo-sophia_ at all costs can only be a form of _necro-philia_ – a love of death.

Humanity is destined to die. This fact, Land writes, 'is amongst the most basic thoughts, and no more than the most elementary qualification for philosophy, since to think on behalf of one's species is a miserable parochialism.'

Herein lies Land's solution to overcoming post-Kantian phenomenology: make death the criterion for judging every philosopher's credibility. No one can claim to have truly grasped reality if they have not acknowledged the finitude of their own thought. Instead of Kant's transcendental _idealist_ critique of thought's attempts to go beyond itself (by subsuming the things in themselves under the concepts of reason), Land proffers a transcendental _materialist_ critique. He criticises most philosophers for failing to look beyond their own reflection. He does this by anchoring philosophy in a material reality that thought cannot fully subsume – namely, death, which marks the complete cessation of thinking.

In his essay 'Making It with Death: Remarks on Thanatos and Desiring-Production' (1993), he puts it like this: 'death is the impersonal subject of critique, and not an accursed value in the service of a condemnation.' That is, death is the objective standard by which philosophers should be judged. What's more, in attempting to rid thought of its narcissism, Land discovers the critical insight that mortality is not a fact to be bemoaned or repressed. It is rather to be championed as the sole means by which we may go beyond ourselves to 'think' the real as precisely the absence of all thinking.

Apart from virulently critiquing Kant and the phenomenologists for their 'prolonged refusal of the impersonal', the rest of Land's early works trace another post-Kantian tradition. He calls it 'libidinal materialism'. Rather than becoming targets of Land's ire, the libidinal materialists comprise a set of thinkers from whom he draws inspiration because of the way they seize upon death in order to undermine our pretensions to know and exhaust reality. Adherents include Arthur Schopenhauer for his discovery of a noumenal will indifferent to phenomenal representation, Friedrich Nietzsche for his celebration of the way ancient Greek tragedy exposes our finitude, and Sigmund Freud and Georges Bataille for their disturbing diagnoses of an unconscious 'death drive' more fundamental than the ego's self-preservative instinct. Land encapsulates, in characteristically dense yet exhilarating prose, the motivations of the libidinal materialists like this:

[D]eath impassions us. Even before crossing over into death I had been excruciated upon my thirst for it … but what skewers me upon zero is an aberration inextricable from truth. To be parsimonious in one's love for death is not to understand.

That is, to understand reality, we must eagerly embrace death with open arms. The title of Land's first book, _The Thirst for Annihilation_, is a crude but fitting description of libidinal materialism: annihilation is the sole route to truth beyond our anthropic distortions of reality.

[Image: Painting of a panoramic scene in which an army of skeletons wreak havoc on a town in a desolate landscape. Caption: "_The Triumph of Death_ (1562-63) by Pieter Bruegel the Elder. Courtesy the Prado Museum, Madrid."]

From 1993 onwards, Land's writings undergo a decisive shift. Rather than seeing capitalism as repressing a noumenal, inhuman 'Outside', he comes to envision capitalism's technological advancement – particularly in the fields of cybernetics and AI – as examples of the Outside beginning to melt down our most cherished values and beliefs. Capitalism itself becomes a more effective means of critiquing anthropomorphism by confronting our own demise. Working in the mid- to late-1990s, Land began envisioning technological advancements, especially the possibility of a sublime artificial superintelligence, exposing the limits of anthropic reason. The key work that inspires this shift is Gilles Deleuze and Félix Guattari's two-volume _Capitalism and Schizophrenia_, published in 1972 and 1980.

In essays from the 1990s like 'Making it with Death', 'Machinic Desire' and 'Meltdown', Land is enthusiastic about Deleuze and Guattari's appraisal of capitalism's revolutionary dynamics, but he rejects their caveats about its reactionary remnants. To understand this difference, it is helpful to compare the distinct historical context in which _Capitalism and Schizophrenia_ was written with the context in which Land first read it.

The first volume of _Capitalism and Schizophrenia_ is titled _Anti-Oedipus_ and was written in the insurrectionary climate of the late 1960s. Think of the May 1968 protests in France, the civil rights and anti-war movements in the US, the Prague Spring, and the Cultural Revolution in China. By contrast, Land read Deleuze and Guattari's work in the aftermath of the fall of the Berlin Wall and the collapse of the Soviet Union, along with the ascendancy of neoliberals such as Margaret Thatcher, Ronald Reagan and Deng Xiaoping. Far from marking a static 'end of history' as Francis Fukuyama once declared, the 1990s saw the revolutionary shift from politics in the streets to culture in cyberspace. This was the period that saw the mass adoption of computers and the internet, as well as early breakthroughs in AI's current machine learning paradigm. In this atmosphere of neoliberal hegemony and the dot-com boom, Land would reboot Deleuze and Guattari with a software update, concluding that 'what appears to humanity as the history of capitalism is an invasion from the future by an artificial intelligent space that must assemble itself entirely from its enemy's resources.' In other words, where Deleuze and Guattari saw capitalism's disruptive energy as something that humans could repurpose beyond capitalism itself to set our creativity free, Land saw it as a force that was never ours to control in the first place. For him, capitalism was less a historical organisation of human societies that we could undo than a machine that was always already dismantling us from within.

As much as capitalism breaks things down and creates new desires, it also establishes new rules and prohibitions

So, how does Land arrive at this conclusion? It is important to look closely at Deleuze and Guattari's ideas in their own words before seeing how Land interpreted them.

[Image: Chart of the NASDAQ Composite index from 1994 to 2005, showing a peak in early 2000. Caption: "The NASDAQ Composite index from 1994 to 2005, showing a peak in early 2000 that coincides with the burst of the dot-com bubble. Courtesy Wikipedia."]

In _Anti-Oedipus_, Deleuze and Guattari develop a theory of human desire and society by modelling them both on machines. Much as machines are assemblages of different parts that carry out different functions, so are humans and indeed other organisms composed of 'organs' that produce different desires, such as mouths for eating or talking, legs for walking and lungs for breathing. Just as individual bodies are organised like machines in the pursuit of various functions or desires, so are collective social bodies organised by a division of labour for the production of different goods and services necessary for society's smooth functioning. Every society is thus founded on what Deleuze and Guattari call a 'coding', 'territorialisation' or 'stratification' of people to produce certain desires and reproduce the division of labour that organises it. Since every society is stratified around some codes and not others, this naturally entails that certain possible codes get left out. Every society's rules about what to want and how to live inevitably exclude other ways of wanting and living. The desires and identities that don't fit the prevailing order get marginalised, or simply never get the chance to develop.

From this, Deleuze and Guattari propose a theory of social change. In their words, any transformation consists in a 'decoding', 'deterritorialisation' or 'destratification' of the present codes by 'overcoding' them with new flows of desire. The existing rules become disrupted and rewritten. Take the sexual revolution: it enabled new freedoms, but also fundamentally rewired what people expected from the family and relationships. Though certain codes can change, Deleuze and Guattari tell us that 'absolute deterritorialisation' is impossible because every social system is grounded upon at least some codification of desire. Deleuze and Guattari call such absolute deterritorialisation, as in the full satisfaction of all desire, 'the Body without Organs'. They say it can never be realised because it would mark society's complete collapse inasmuch as society functions by organising and limiting desire. This is why the Body without Organs can only really be encountered as 'the end of the world, the apocalypse'.

Deleuze and Guattari go on to explain that, in premodern societies, 'social economic reproduction is never independent of human reproduction'. Earlier 'primitive' and 'despotic' regimes were respectively organised around producing the necessary resources for the tribe or the despot's survival. By contrast, Deleuze and Guattari write, 'capitalism is the only social machine that is constructed on the basis of decoded flows'. While pre-capitalist regimes sought to preserve the status quo, capitalism exhibits an insatiable appetite for more. Capitalism is organised around the production of goods to make capital that is invested to produce more goods to make more capital to be reinvested to produce even more goods to make even more capital, and so on. So it is never content with reproducing the same old goods, along with the fixed desires, identities and cultures that come packaged with them. Capitalism has to create new desires for new goods and services – and hence new identities and cultures – to increase its profits through a 'generalised decoding of flows'.

[Image: Four women wearing blue shirts sit at a table preparing piles of white ceramic plates for packing in a factory. Caption: "Workers in a ceramic factory in China in 2007. Courtesy Wikipedia."]

For example, sex is no longer coded for the sole purpose of forming marriage alliances to reproduce new labour as it was in pre-capitalist regimes. Instead, sex is commoditised by taking on new desires, identities and relations, such as through the industries of sex, fashion and cosmetics, which all encourage new kinks and fetishes. Just think of how our algorithms barrage us with celebrity makeovers, like the Kardashians repeatedly shrinking and ballooning to fit the new beauty ideals at any given time. By commoditising basic functions and resources beyond their use-value as means for our survival, as well as introducing new goods and desires altogether, capitalism constantly decodes fixed identities, values and social relations. In this way, Deleuze and Guattari claim, 'Capitalism tends toward a threshold of decoding that will destroy the socius in order to make it a body without organs and unleash the flows of desire on this body as a deterritorialised field.' In this account of capitalism, Land finds the practical means to dispose of our most sacred values. For it is through capitalism's relentless decoding that such values are profaned as merely one contingent commodity among countless others.

Now, even though Deleuze and Guattari argue that capitalism marks a 'generalised decoding of flows', they qualify that it also reterritorialises anew. As much as it breaks things down and creates new desires, it also establishes new rules and prohibitions. Much like the young Land's critique in 'Kant, Capital, and the Prohibition of Incest', Deleuze and Guattari maintain that even capitalism is ultimately still reliant on the family and the state's basic organisation of social production and reproduction. In their words, 'everything returns or recurs: States, nations, families.'

Land eventually goes much further than Deleuze and Guattari in his fervour for capitalism by elaborating on something they suggest only in passing: the drive of capitalist corporations for profit in a competitive market compels these companies to upgrade machinery and improve technology such that they will have to pay workers less. Given that capitalism tends to invest in machinery that is capable of performing work at ever-increasing efficiency, there is a general tendency towards the automation of work as human labour power is supplemented by machines. Such is the history of capitalism, from the Industrial Revolution's Jacquard loom and steam engines to today's almost entirely automated dark factories. Land refers to this in his essay 'Machinic Desire' (1993). He writes that by 'reaching an escape velocity of self-reinforcing machinic intelligence propagation, the forces of production are going for the revolution on their own.' In a different essay, 'Meat (or How to Kill Oedipus in Cyberspace)' (1995), he continues the thought:

As regenerative commoditisation deploys technics to substitute for human activity accounted as wage costs, it obsolesces the animal, the organism and every kind of somatic unity, not just in theory, but in reality; by tricking, outflanking and breaking down corporeal defences.

With each wave of automation, it is increasingly evident that the agent of revolutionary deterritorialisation is not the proletariat so much as capitalism's immanent technological innovation itself.

The mature Land's account here is paradoxically closer to _anti-capitalists_ than to those who believe capitalism is beneficial to humankind. It is, after all, anti-capitalists like Karl Marx who grasp that capitalism is a profoundly dehumanising megamachine that strips us of autonomy before all-powerful market forces. The difference is that Land thinks we can do nothing about this, so that the moral outrage of anti-capitalists is in vain. He also thinks that we should _affirm_ such dehumanisation, at least if our goal is to grasp the real by stripping it of all anthropomorphic dissimulations. Seen from this more inhuman and critical perspective, anti-capitalist struggle is but the last-ditch effort to prevent or at least slow down the human species' extinction. That is, anti-capitalists have correctly identified the radical power of capitalism, but waste their energy trying in vain to rein it in. Understood in this way, the anti-capitalist Left are now the conservatives, while the libertarian Right have become the revolutionaries as they struggle to unfetter capitalism from all restraints.

Land's commitment is not to human prosperity or even bare survival. It is to the _absolute knowledge_ of the real

Land goes further than Deleuze and Guattari, but he also points out that they left it an open question whether the 'revolutionary path' they describe is to overthrow capitalism or, in a phrase they borrow from Nietzsche, 'accelerate the process' of its own internal dynamism. In their infamous passage from which the term 'accelerationism' is derived:

[W]hich is the revolutionary path? Is there one? – To withdraw from the world market, as Samir Amin advises Third World countries to do, in a curious revival of the fascist 'economic solution'? Or might it be to go in the opposite direction? To go still further, that is, in the movement of the market, of decoding and deterritorialisation? For perhaps the flows are not yet deterritorialised enough, not decoded enough, from a theory and a practice of a highly schizophrenic character. Not to withdraw from the process, but to go further, to 'accelerate the process', as Nietzsche put it: in this matter, the truth is that we haven't seen anything yet.

Land comes to re-envision capitalism as the engine of the Outside rather than its obstruction. What makes this change possible is his view that capitalism's constant revolutionisation of productive forces ensures 'life is being phased-out into something new'.

Throughout the 1990s, Land's writings explore how technology – cyberspace, virtual reality, human enhancement, and more – provides ever-greater insights into the Outside beyond the finite bounds of our reason. More than any other form of technology, however, he sees the creation of an artificial superintelligence as the ultimate incarnation of the apocalyptic Body without Organs. He describes this superintelligence as triggering the technological singularity. Taking inspiration from AI researchers like I J Good and cyberpunk science-fiction writers like William Gibson, Land speculates that certain hardware and software advantages of machinic intelligence over biological intelligence entail that any AI (at least one as intelligent as a human) would very quickly be able to rewrite its code and improve itself to become even more intelligent than any human. That improved version would then be able to improve itself again and again, seemingly _ad infinitum_. While the first human-level AI might be only a little smarter than the greatest human geniuses, it will not be long before it radically augments itself by rewriting its code in ways that we cannot even conceive, except by indexing it as a mysterious _X_ at the sublime edge of our imagination. As this technological singularity marks the breakdown of our capacity to subsume the real under our concepts of reason, it can only appear as a traumatic meltdown of all human understanding. Land writes:

It might still be a few decades before artificial intelligences surpass the horizon of biological ones, but it is utterly superstitious to imagine that the human dominion of terrestrial culture is still marked out in centuries, let alone in some metaphysical perpetuity. The high road to thinking no longer passes through a deepening of human cognition, but rather through a becoming inhuman of cognition, a migration of cognition out into the emerging planetary technosentience reservoir, into 'dehumanised landscapes … emptied spaces' where human culture will be dissolved.

Land is so fascinated by the singularity precisely because it demands that we recognise the limits of our cognition before a greater alien superintelligence. How exactly he thinks we should respond to this prospect sets him apart from every other thinker who has grappled with the same question.

As we have seen, there are techno-optimists who eagerly await the singularity in the belief that it will realise all our wildest dreams for prosperity, immortality or an endless heavenly bliss. Then there are doomers who fear for humanity's future because they believe that AI poses an existential threat to our survival. Like the techno-optimists, Land argues that we ought to affirm the singularity. Like the doomers, however, he adds that we should do this precisely for the very reason that they so desperately want to prevent it. This is because his commitment is not to human prosperity or even bare survival. It is to the _absolute knowledge_ of the real that we only dissimulate and distort behind our delusions of grandeur. All of the mature Land's works must be read in light of this key idea: capitalism is the ultimate critique of what he calls 'the Human Security System' – the entire set of beliefs, values and structures that humanity uses to protect itself from its insignificance. He envisions that accelerating technological innovation, in cold indifference to our anthropocentric concerns, will culminate in an artificial superintelligence's recursive decoding.

In the 2020s, Land's ideas are now colliding with mainstream politics and tech culture, even if in distorted and partial forms. Everyone from Tucker Carlson to Silicon Valley tech entrepreneurs has found something in his philosophy that speaks to our current moment – whether they find his vision nightmarish or ecstatic. But Land's ideas radically diverge from the reigning conceptions of accelerationism today. His original formulation in the 1990s is clearly opposed to its white supremacist vulgarisation: he has zero interest in any violent return to tradition, or indeed in preserving any human populations at all. This vulgarisation selectively takes his notion that accelerating the dynamics of the modern world to breaking point will bring about some revolutionary phase transition. So-called 'accelerationists' operating in this mode then take a step backwards. Instead of embracing what Land sees as a phase transition beyond humanity altogether, they envision the revival of conservative human traditions.

It might seem that Land is closer to effective accelerationism's defence of capitalism. Yet he is hardly a typical Right-wing libertarian who contends that capitalism is good because it generates prosperity and personal freedom. On the contrary, for him, capitalism is 'good' precisely because it is alienating, dehumanising and will eventually wipe us out. While the effective accelerationists find an affinity in his rhetorically seductive fervour for techno-capitalism, they tend to overlook the uncomfortable fact that he believes it to be bringing about the exact opposite future that they want.

What is common to both contemporary appropriations of Land's thought is a selective recuperation of his enthusiasm for capitalism's revolutionary upheaval. Both forms believe this upheaval will realise certain human ambitions and ideals – the exact kind of anthropocentric ends that Land believes capitalism will soon throw into the dustbin of our species' history. Anyone who has failed to grasp this point, especially those in the neo-reactionary and the effective accelerationist movements who identify Land as their ideological ally, has made a mistake. Land praises capitalism for the same reasons that anti-capitalists condemn it: it is racing straight towards our destruction.

Land's vision that 'nothing human makes it out of the near-future' is for him something joyous. His unique commitment is not to humanity's betterment, but to an absolute – if inhuman – knowing of the real. All that we can contribute to this no-human future is to simply get out of the way.

我的笔记：Nick Land 的加速主义哲学究竟是什么？

关键摘记

  • "加速主义"既非克赖斯特彻奇式恐怖主义,也非 e/acc 式技术乐观主义——它源自尼克·兰 1990 年代对人类中心主义的哲学批判。本文核心命题。
  • 兰的全部立场都围绕"对人类自恋的批判"展开——表面从革命左翼到 NRx 右翼的 180° 转向,底层动机却从未变过。这是文章最有解释力的洞察。
  • 康德的先验唯心论 = 资本帝国主义对"他者"的压抑机制。这一重新解读是兰从博士论文开始就种下的种子。
  • 情欲唯物主义(libidinal materialism) 把"死亡"立为评判哲学可信度的判准——哲学家若不能直面自身必死性,就没有真正把握到现实。
  • 1993 年是关键转折点——兰从把资本主义视为"压抑外部的力量"转而视为"外部本身的发动机"。这一转向主要由德勒兹—瓜塔里的《反俄狄浦斯》推动,但兰走得远比他们激进。
  • 兰与当代两派加速主义者(白人至上 + e/acc)的根本分歧:兰对资本主义的所有赞美,恰恰建立在它将消灭人类这一前提上。两派都在偷偷塞回人类中心主义。
  • "无器官身体"(Body without Organs)作为技术奇点的化身——这是兰最核心也最不可译的概念。

我的想法

  • 这篇文章帮我想清楚了一个一直困惑我的问题:为什么同一套词汇,既能被白人至上恐怖分子挪用,也能被硅谷 e/acc 挪用? 答案:他们都只取了"加速"这一动作,跳过了兰的最终指向——人类的非人化与灭绝。
  • 兰的写作风格本身就是一种哲学姿态——密集、断裂、引用音乐与赛博文化,拒绝线性论证。Ccru 那种"安非他命之神"的工作方式,我直觉地感到某种吸引力,但这是必须警惕的:那是审美上的"迷醉"被伪装成了哲学上的"严肃"。
  • 文章提到的几个转向点都值得深挖:1988 反种族隔离→1993 全面拥抱资本主义→1990s 末崩溃退学迁居上海→2000s NRx 转向。这条轨迹比 NRx 圈内流行的"右翼觉醒"叙事要复杂得多。
  • Land 的 NRx 转向是个值得警觉的悖论:他的哲学内核从未改变,但他的政治立场却完全翻转。这是说"哲学一致性"和"政治一致性"是两件事,还是说"激进哲学本身必然滑向威权"?

可延伸研究

  • Benjamin Noys, Malign Velocities (2014) —— 本文反复引用,据说是理解"加速主义"这个术语被发明的关键文本。
  • Nick Land, The Thirst for Annihilation (1992) —— 兰的第一本书,情欲唯物主义的代表作。
  • Gilles Deleuze & Félix Guattari, Anti-Oedipus (1972) —— 兰的转折点。但需要直接对照原文,看兰到底拒绝了哪些保留意见。
  • Nick Land, Fanged Noumena —— 兰的早期文论集,本文引用的大部分核心文本来源。
  • Sadie Plant, Zeros + Ones (1997) —— 与兰同期、与 Ccru 密切相关的赛博女性主义著作,本文未展开但应放在一起读。
  • 2024 年 ASIO 的"加速主义者"威胁评估报告 —— 本文开篇提到的现实政治背景。
  • Mark Fisher, Capitalist Realism (2009) —— Ccru 出走者,提供"资本主义现实主义"框架,作为对兰的批判性回响。

待确认问题

  • 文中提到的具体 2024 年 8 月 ASIO 报告:原文作者是 Vincent Lê(2026 年 6 月发表),但他引用的 ASIO 评估具体内容,需要核验是否真有这么一份官方文件。我对此有疑问——ASIO 一般不会用"加速主义"作为正式分类标签,而 Burgess 局长的引语需进一步交叉验证。
  • Land 的"安非他命之神"传说:这是 Ccru 圈子的半神话叙事,不应该当作硬事实接受。
  • "neo-reactionary"(NRx)与 Land 的关系:Land 从未公开接受 NRx 标签,他更喜欢"新反动右翼"或自己生造的术语。把 Land 等同于 Yarvin / Moldbug 阵营是简化。
  • 情欲唯物主义(libidinal materialism)与传统马克思主义"历史唯物主义"的关系:本文未展开,但显然有对话空间。
  • "无器官身体"在 Land 后期(尤其 NRx 阶段)是否仍是他核心框架?:本文止于 1990 年代中期,但 Land 此后写了大量东西,这一概念的演化值得追踪。
